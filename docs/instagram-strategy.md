# AppVibe Studio — Instagram Strategy & Content System

> Status: Ready for agent implementation
> Audience: Indonesian SME owners, professional-service businesses, growing teams, clinics/health/beauty, property/construction, education/event businesses
> Primary goal: turn Instagram attention into qualified website inquiries, not follower vanity.
> Source of truth: current AppVibe repo (`main`) + target-customer pain research. Portfolio claims must remain labeled as demo/simulation where applicable.

## 1. Strategic thesis

AppVibe should NOT behave like a generic web-design catalogue. Instagram should make business owners recognize a costly problem, teach them how a better digital presence solves it, demonstrate AppVibe's thinking through real demos/case studies, then move high-intent prospects to the AppVibe website and WhatsApp.

Core positioning:

> AppVibe helps growing businesses turn their digital presence into a clearer, more credible path from attention to inquiry.

Content promise:

> We don't just show websites. We explain why a business website works — positioning, trust, information architecture, proof, CTA, and lead flow.

Funnel:

Instagram content → profile visit → bio/link → AppVibe website → portfolio/demo → WhatsApp/inquiry → qualified lead.

## 2. Target-customer pain research

### 2.1 Cross-segment pain map

| No measurement | Owner cannot tell which channel creates leads | "Traffic bukan tujuan akhir" | Lead dashboard concept |

Research supports the strategic direction but should not be turned into unsupported numerical claims in content. Indonesian SME research identifies perceived usefulness, resources, ease of use, and social influence as adoption barriers; recent Indonesian reporting also highlights device/internet constraints. Professional-services research shows buyers commonly inspect a firm's website before choosing a provider, while B2B research emphasizes transparency, case studies/testimonials, and immediate access to information. Treat these as strategic evidence, not as promises about every AppVibe prospect.

Sources used in research:
- Hinge: professional-services buyers use multiple research methods and over 80% visit the firm's website when evaluating a provider.
- Bank Indonesia / Katadata reporting: digital-adoption barriers among Indonesian micro/small businesses include technology/resource constraints.
- Indonesian SME research: perceived usefulness and resources are major resistance factors to website/online-store adoption.
- Clutch B2B research: buyers value transparent cost information, case studies/testimonials, and immediate access to information.

## 3. Segment-specific pain

### Professional services: consultants, contractors, agencies, B2B vendors, interior, legal
- Reputation is hard to communicate through an Instagram grid alone.
- Prospects need to understand capability, specialization, examples, process, and next step before contacting.
- Referral traffic can die when the digital footprint looks weak.
- Owner/team repeatedly explains the same introductory information.

### Webinar / education / event businesses
- Promotion often becomes poster + caption + WhatsApp broadcast.
- Benefits and agenda are unclear.
- Registrant data is scattered across chats.
- No focused landing URL for campaigns.
- Follow-up is manual and inconsistent.

### Clinic / health / beauty
- Trust and clarity are more important than flashy visuals.
- Visitors need services, experts, schedule/booking, FAQ, and a safe contact path.
- Content must avoid exaggerated medical claims.
- Dummy case-study content must stay explicitly simulated.

### Property / developer / agent / construction / renovation / interior
- Social posts show properties but do not create a structured catalogue.
- Prospects need type, location, status, specifications, gallery, and inquiry/survey flow.
- Ads and brochures need a single destination that continues the buying journey.

### Growing sales teams / SMEs with multiple lead channels
- Leads become scattered across WhatsApp, Instagram and forms.
- Owners cannot see source, stage, follow-up or team ownership.
- Reporting is manual.
- A website is useful, but the next step is lead-system visibility.

## 4. Content pillars

Use five pillars. Target monthly mix: 35% Problem Awareness, 25% Education/Teardown, 20% Proof/Portfolio, 10% Build/Founder, 10% Offer/Conversion.

### P1 — Problem Recognition
Make the owner say: "Ini masalah bisnis saya."

### P2 — Website & Conversion Education
Explain positioning, hierarchy, CTA, trust, proof, information architecture, mobile UX, inquiry friction.

### P3 — Proof / Portfolio / Demo
Show the thinking behind each AppVibe demo, not just screenshots.

### P4 — Build / Founder / Process
Show how AppVibe researches, structures, designs, builds and iterates.

### P5 — Offer / Conversion
Explain who AppVibe is for, when a website is worth it, what the process looks like, and invite qualified conversations.

## 5. Format system

### Reels
Formula: Hook (0–2s) → problem visualization (2–7s) → diagnosis (7–18s) → solution/demo (18–35s) → CTA (last 3–5s).

Rules:
- Start with the business problem, not the AppVibe logo.
- Use screen recordings of actual demos whenever possible.
- One idea per Reel.
- Add large on-screen Indonesian text.
- Cut dead time aggressively.
- Show the result/structure before explaining details.

### Carousel
Formula: Painful statement → why it happens → 3–5 diagnostic points → better structure → AppVibe example → CTA.

Rules:
- Slide 1 must work as a standalone hook.
- One message per slide.
- Prefer diagrams, UI crops, annotations and comparison frames over generic stock imagery.
- End with a useful action, not a generic "follow for more".

### Stories
Use daily for low-production interaction:
- poll a business problem
- show build progress
- preview a demo
- answer FAQ
- share new portfolio
- invite DM keyword

### Qualification
Ask for: business type, current digital channels, primary goal, city/market, approximate scope/budget when appropriate. Do not promise outcomes.

## 9. Content rules for agent

1. Never invent a real client, testimonial, revenue result, conversion lift, lead count, or business outcome.
2. If a portfolio brand/data is dummy, say "demo", "simulasi", or "skenario" in the content when confusion is possible.
3. Do not present 68 sample projects, 50 leads, 12 lead details, 6 property listings, etc. as real client data. They are mock/demo data in the repo.
4. Do not make medical claims for the clinic demo.
5. Do not turn the technology stack into the main sales message.
6. Avoid generic "tips digital marketing" unless directly tied to a business problem AppVibe solves.
7. Avoid AI-slop visuals: generic robots, neon brains, floating laptops, random 3D business people, meaningless dashboards.
8. Prefer real AppVibe UI crops, browser recordings, annotated screenshots, diagrams, typography-led slides, and clean editorial layouts.
9. One post = one argument.
10. The first 1–2 seconds / first carousel slide must communicate the tension.
11. Every post must have one primary CTA.
12. Optimize for shares, saves, profile visits, link clicks, qualified DMs, WhatsApp conversations and leads — not likes alone.

## 14. Content production workflow for an AI agent

Input: portfolio entry + target segment + selected hook.

Step 1 — Extract one pain, one desired outcome, one proof element.
Step 2 — Select format using the format matrix.
Step 3 — Write hook first. Reject generic hooks.
Step 4 — Build the narrative around one argument.
Step 5 — Pull visuals from the actual AppVibe demo/case-study whenever possible.
Step 6 — Write caption with context, useful insight, one CTA.
Step 7 — Check claims against portfolio source data.
Step 8 — Mark simulated/dummy content clearly where needed.
Step 9 — Produce accessibility text / on-screen copy.
Step 10 — Assign funnel stage and KPI.

## 18. Agent acceptance criteria

A generated post is rejected when:
- hook is generic and could belong to any marketing agency;
- it sells "professional website" without identifying a concrete business problem;
- it invents proof/results;
- it uses dummy data as real data;
- it uses medical claims for clinic content;
- it has multiple competing CTAs;
- the visual does not connect to the argument;
- caption is a transcript of the video instead of adding context;
- it ends with an empty "DM us" without a reason to act.

A generated post passes when:
- the target owner can recognize the pain in seconds;
- one clear idea is taught or demonstrated;
- actual AppVibe proof is shown when relevant;
- the CTA matches funnel stage;
- claims are traceable to repo data or clearly labeled inference;
- the content can be understood without sound;
- the post strengthens AppVibe's positioning as a strategic digital partner, not just a website vendor.
